# Milestone 5 — Synthesis: escalate the predictor, fund the cliff

## Verdict first: M4 wins, M3 sets the roadmap

If the CFO has one product team and one customer success team, the finding to
escalate **first is M4's: companies that evaluate hard but never invite a
teammate churn at 36% vs ~21% baseline.** CS gets the watchlist next week;
product gets M3's onboarding redesign as the funded longer bet. That is a
commitment, not a hedge — one team acts now on a named list, the other builds
for the quarter. What follows is the scoring that earns it.

## The criteria (declared before scoring)

1. **Size of effect** — how much churn does this explain or prevent?
2. **Ease of action** — can a team act on it next week with existing tooling?
3. **Organizational fit** — which team is already configured for this motion?
4. **Speed of feedback** — how fast do we know if we were right?

## Scoring

**Size.** M3 describes the bigger absolute phenomenon: ~38% of every paid
cohort is gone by month 2, and the Mar–May 2026 uptick is broad-based across
29 cohorts. M4's rule covers a smaller slice — ~19% of onboardings (58/302)
carry the flag at 1.7× risk (36.2% vs 20.5%, a 15.7-point gap). On size alone,
M3 wins: nothing in M4 moves the topline as far as fixing month one would.

**Ease of action.** M3's "fix onboarding" is a direction, not an action. M4's
is a trigger rule on data we already log: day-30 check, flag
`dashboard/export AND zero invites`, CS runs an invite assist. One query, one
playbook page. M4 wins by a mile.

**Organizational fit.** M3 asks roadmap-locked product to redirect; M4 asks
customer success — already built around behavioral triggers and save plays —
to do more of what it does. No reorg, no new tooling. M4 wins.

**Speed of feedback.** M4 tests in one monthly cohort: today's live watchlist
is 18 companies, read out against the 20.5% baseline in 60–90 days (~3 extra
saves per cohort, ~35/year). M3's redesign needs a quarter of product work
plus a full cohort lifetime to read. M4 wins.

Three criteria to one. **Escalate M4 first.**

## Why M3 still matters (sequenced, not shelved)

M4 triages; M3 cures. The predictor catches tire-kickers one by one, but the
cliff — 38% gone by month 2 in *every* cohort — is a structural tax on growth
that no save play will repeal. And M3 answered the CFO's actual question (no,
no single bad cohort; the uptick is systemic), which reframes the product
roadmap around first-month value instead of tenured-account retention. So:
CS runs the M4 playbook starting now, and its save-call notes become the
qualitative feed for product's onboarding redesign — the two investigations
compose into a pipeline, with M4 first.
